What Size Chandelier Do I Need Over My Dining Table?

A simple measuring guide for choosing a chandelier that suits your table, ceiling and dining room in a UAE home.

A chandelier can make a dining area feel finished, but the wrong size can block the view across the table or disappear against a tall ceiling. Measure the table first. Then check the fixture’s width, shape and hanging height in the room.

Measure the table, not only the room

For a round fixture above a dining table, a useful starting range is about half to three-quarters of the table’s width. For example, a 100-centimetre-wide table could suit a chandelier around 50 to 75 centimetres wide. The exact choice depends on the fixture’s visual weight, the chairs and how open the room feels.

For a long rectangular table, look at a linear or elongated chandelier that follows the table shape. Kichler’s suggests a linear fixture length around one-third to one-half of the table length as an initial guide. Compare the overall fixture dimensions, including any arms or shades, before ordering.

Choose a comfortable hanging height

As a starting point, keep the bottom of the chandelier about 75 to 90 centimetres above the tabletop. Kichler recommends about 76 centimetres for a round or square fixture over a table, with a little more room for a wider table or higher ceiling. Other guides use a range that reaches about 90 centimetres. These are starting points, not fixed rules for every room.

Have someone sit at the table while another person holds a cardboard outline or temporary marker at the proposed height. Check that people can see each other comfortably and that the fixture does not feel too low. A tall ceiling may allow a larger fixture, but it does not always mean the chandelier should be raised far above the table; the light still needs to feel connected to the dining area.

Match the light to the way you dine

Look at the number and type of lamps, the shade material and whether light shines upward, downward or in every direction. A chandelier may be the main light over a small dining table, or it may work together with ceiling lights and wall lighting in a larger room. If you use a dimmer, confirm that the light source and driver are compatible with it.

For a majlis or a large family dining room, sit in the usual places and check the view from each seat. A broad or open chandelier can feel lighter in the space than a dense design of the same width. Warm finishes, clear glass and alabaster also affect the appearance of light on faces, tableware and wall finishes.

Measure before you order

  1. Write down the table’s length and width.
  2. Mark the chandelier’s width or length on paper or cardboard.
  3. Check the remaining space at each end and along the sides of the table.
  4. Test the hanging height from seated eye level and confirm the ceiling fixing can support the chosen fitting.
